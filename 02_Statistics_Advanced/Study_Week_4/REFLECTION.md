# 📌 Day 18 Reflection — Chi-Square Testing

Day 18 introduced Chi-Square Testing and categorical data analysis.

I learned how Chi-Square tests determine whether relationships exist between categorical variables.

Key concepts learned included:

- observed frequency
- expected frequency
- categorical association
- independence testing
- contingency tables

The Chi-Square formula helped me understand how statistical testing measures deviations between actual and expected outcomes.

\[
\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}
\]

Where:

- \(O\) = Observed Frequency
- \(E\) = Expected Frequency

This day improved my understanding of how businesses analyze relationships between customer behavior, operational categories, and service outcomes.

I also became more confident interpreting categorical statistical results.

# 📌 Day 19 Reflection — Normal Distribution & Z-Score

Day 19 introduced the Normal Distribution and Z-score analysis.

I learned how data distributions help analysts understand:

- average behavior
- unusual observations
- variability
- probability positioning

The Normal Distribution showed how many real-world datasets naturally cluster around the mean.

I also learned how Z-scores standardize values for comparison.

### Z-Score Formula

\[
Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}
\]

Where:

- \(X\) = actual value
- \(\mu\) = mean
- \(\sigma\) = standard deviation

This day improved my ability to:

- identify outliers
- standardize observations
- compare values across datasets
- interpret deviations from the mean

I also understood how Z-scores are used in:

- risk analysis
- anomaly detection
- financial analytics
- operational monitoring
