## 📊 Day 11 — Advanced Correlation Analysis

This lesson introduced a deeper understanding of relationships between variables.

I learned:
- The difference between Pearson and Spearman correlation
- Linear vs monotonic relationships
- How scatter plots help validate relationship structure
- Why correlation methods must be selected carefully

One major breakthrough was understanding that:

> Correlation is not just about calculation — it is also about validating relationship reliability and structure.

This lesson expanded my understanding beyond basic correlation analysis from earlier lessons.

---

## 📊 Day 12 — Covariance & Variance Relationships

This was one of the most challenging lessons of the week.

I initially struggled with:
- Interpreting large covariance values
- Understanding why covariance differs from correlation
- Why variance and standard deviation are important

Eventually, I understood:
- Covariance measures movement direction
- Correlation standardizes relationship strength
- Variance measures volatility
- Standard deviation measures spread in original units

This lesson improved my understanding of how business metrics move together and how analysts measure variability across multiple variables.

---

## 📊 Day 13 — Sampling & Sampling Bias

This was probably the most conceptually difficult lesson of the week.

The lesson focused on:
- Population vs sample representation
- Sampling methods
- Sampling bias
- Reliability assessment

One major challenge was understanding why sample percentages did not exactly match population percentages.

Through repeated analysis and interpretation, I learned:
- Samples rarely perfectly match populations
- Small differences are acceptable
- Bias must be measured, not assumed
- Representation reliability is more important than exact equality

I also learned why stratified sampling is often the most reliable approach for preserving subgroup representation.

---

## 📊 Day 14 — Confidence Intervals

This lesson introduced uncertainty estimation and population range analysis.

I learned:
- How confidence intervals estimate realistic population ranges
- Why analysts should avoid relying solely on sample averages
- How interval width reflects uncertainty and precision
- Why wider intervals indicate greater variability

One important realization from this lesson was:

> Analysts estimate realistic ranges, not exact population values.

This significantly improved my understanding of statistical reliability and estimation precision.

---

## 📊 Day 15 — Hypothesis Testing Fundamentals

This was the most advanced lesson of the week and introduced statistical decision-making.

I learned:
- How to formulate null and alternative hypotheses
- How p-values measure statistical evidence
- Why statistical significance matters
- Why observed business improvements must be tested rather than assumed

Using a paired t-test, I tested whether marketing campaigns significantly improved average daily sales.

The analysis showed extremely strong statistical evidence that campaign activity significantly increased sales performance.

This lesson introduced one of the most important analytical principles:

> Analysts must distinguish between random fluctuation and statistically meaningful business change.
